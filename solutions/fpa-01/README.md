## The brief

It is the third working day of October. The ledger for September has landed, the plan has not moved since January, and the head of finance wants one page before the Thursday meeting: what we said, what happened, and why. Build it in a spreadsheet somebody else could pick up and refresh next month without asking you a single question.

**Scope:** A spreadsheet, plus a short memo. Uses only this level: imports, SUMIFS, a lookup, a check cell, one chart, and words. No macros, no Python, no add-ins.

## Why the solution is shaped this way

- The deliverable is a spreadsheet, because level 1 is a spreadsheet level and the interface finance actually runs on is a workbook. It is generated by a script so that the structure can be asserted rather than described, which is the only way a claim like "nothing is hardcoded" means anything.
- Six tabs in the order data flows: the two raw imports untouched, a quality tab, a mapping tab, one calculations tab, and the pack. The calculations tab is the boundary: the sign rule, the text repair and the duplicate handling all happen there once, and nothing downstream repeats any of it.
- Variances are signed so that positive is always favourable to profit, decided by the account group rather than by the line name. Half the lines in a profit and loss are favourable when they go up and half when they go down, and a pack that leaves the reader to work out which is a pack that will be misread out loud in a meeting.
- The check cell is built from two different routes: the pack side adds up the lines in the layout, and the ledger side adds up every row for the month by its group. That is what makes it catch the realistic failure, where an account is mapped and never added to the pack, rather than comparing a number to itself.
- The duplicate journal is excluded with a first_time column rather than by deleting a row, so the raw import stays exactly as the export wrote it and the exclusion is visible and reversible. JE000261, 590,000 of June payroll, counted once, and reported to the accountant rather than quietly dropped.
- The workbook checker has a --prove flag that pastes the correct value over one pack cell and asserts the checker catches it. A check that has never failed might be checking nothing, and the pasted number being right this month is exactly why it is dangerous.
- What could not be done is stated rather than implied: no spreadsheet engine on this machine evaluates the formulas, so the shape is proved by opening the file and reading them, and the values are proved by computing the same pack a second way in plain Python. The README says which of the two claims each method supports.

## Where people get stuck

| Symptom | Cause |
|---------|-------|
| The pack total is negative on a profitable month | Revenue is a credit and exports negative. Normalise the sign once, where the data arrives. |
| A SUM is exactly 590,000 too high | A round, exact, single line difference is a duplicated export row. Count rows against distinct journal ids. |
| A cost line is missing from the total and nothing warns you | An amount stored as text. The sum skips it silently, and only the check cell notices. |
| One account appears twice in a group by | A trailing space in the label. Group on the code, and trim on the way in. |
| A budget line has no actuals and reads as a saving | Cost centre CC600 was budgeted and never created. Show it and say so: nothing was saved. |
| The numbers were right last month and wrong this month | A SUMIFS range that stops where the data stopped when it was written, or a pasted value over a formula. |

## Self-checks the solution satisfies

- The check cell reads zero, and deleting one row from the mapping tab turns it red
- Changing the month cell from 2025-09 to 2025-08 moves the entire pack, with no other edit
- September revenue is 3,361,050 and EBITDA is 265,989
- The price and volume bridge sums to the transaction fee variance of +151,993
- Every variance in the pack is signed so that a positive number is favourable to profit
- The data quality tab names the duplicated journal id and says whether the pack includes it
- No cell in the pack contains a typed number except the month, the materiality threshold, and labels
- The commentary names a cause and a consequence for each of the three largest variances
- A reader who has never seen the file can follow any pack number back to a ledger row in three clicks
